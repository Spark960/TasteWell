# Tastewell visual system

Tastewell is a curated field guide for practical hospitality decisions. It should feel clear enough for an operator at work and considered enough to be worth revisiting after a pilot.

## Surface

- Primary route: `/`, the programming decision workspace. `/decision` remains a direct link to the same workspace.
- Secondary route: `/compare`, the original grounded versus generic brief demonstration.
- Mode: operate. Dense evidence, editable plans, and changing states take priority over decoration.

## Visual language

- Warm paper (`#f7f5ef`) is the main workspace ground; deep forest (`#163c34`) anchors navigation and primary actions. Ink is `#1f2d29`, muted ink `#586b63`, coral `#cf684e` marks attention, and pale sage (`#dce8db`) supports evidence labels.
- Newsreader is used sparingly for large editorial headings. DM Sans carries controls, data, and body copy. Numbered and tabular data use tabular figures.
- The signature is a decision trail: input, options, pilot, and outcome remain legible as connected stages, while source counts and evidence IDs are small but findable.
- Surfaces use clear rules, spacing, and occasional tinted grounds. Inputs and buttons remain standard web controls. Results read as distinct records rather than a grid of identical cards.
- Focus rings, selection, scrollbars, empty states, loading, errors, and reduced motion use the same palette.

## Content and behavior

Keep all operational caveats: Qloo affinity is aggregate, ranks are scoped to their own venue call, and costs, booking, attendance, and causal impact need operator evidence. No fictitious customers, outcomes, or success claims.
